# Being found, and marketing without the cringe

The owner's priority (22 Oct 2026): being easy to find on the App Store comes first, then some honest marketing.

## 1. App Store search (ASO) comes first
Most downloads of a utility come from App Store search. What counts, in order:
1. **Name (30 characters):** the strongest signal. Use the brand plus what it is, such as *Glass Clock: Widget Studio*.
2. **Subtitle (30):** the next strongest. Use the words people actually type, such as *Aesthetic Home Screen Widgets*.
3. **Keyword field (100):** hidden from people, read by search. Never repeat words already in the name or subtitle (they count once anyway). No spaces after commas, and singular words cover plurals. Draft, 98 characters:
   `digital,desk,time,date,font,custom,minimal,liquid,transparent,frosted,theme,setup,big,simple,color`
4. **The first three screenshots:** they decide whether a searcher taps. Show the result (a beautiful Home Screen), not the settings. See `04-screenshots-plan.md`.
5. **Ratings:** a steady trickle of real ones. Use Apple's review prompt after a happy moment (the first Apply), never on launch.
6. **Localise the listing:** the UK and US App Stores each get their own keyword field, so the second one is a free 100 characters. Spanish, German, French and Japanese listings later reach whole new searches.
7. **Custom product pages and in-app events:** a page per audience (for example "aesthetic setup" or "desk clock"), and an event for each big update, which appear in search.

## 2. Friends and reviews
Friends **testing** the app is great, through TestFlight before launch: honest feedback, bugs found early. Friends leaving reviews because they're friends counts as review manipulation under Apple's rules, and Apple removes such reviews. If a friend genuinely uses it and wants to review it after launch, that's fine. Don't script it or ask everyone on launch day.

## 3. Marketing: creative director, not full-time poster
The idea of mostly-automatic social accounts, with the owner setting the message and watching the numbers, can work if what's posted is real. What tends to work for a widget app:
- **The product is visual.** Short screen recordings of a real Home Screen with the clock on it, and a 10-second "making a style" clip. No talking needed.
- **Where people already look:** Home Screen setup communities (TikTok and Instagram "homescreen setup", Reddit r/iOSsetups and r/iPad) and Home Screen creators who'd enjoy a free, good-looking widget.
- **Build in public:** "a designer making their first app with AI" is a real, likeable story, and it's already on the ideas list.
- **Batch it:** make 10–15 clips in one session a month, schedule them, then check what worked.
- **Measure:** App Store Connect shows impressions, product page views, downloads and where they came from. Give each social account its own campaign link (App Store Connect → App Analytics → Campaigns) to see which one brings installs.

## 4. Not doing
Ads in the app, paid reviews, fake urgency, "rate 5 stars to unlock", buying followers.
